How it was built →
"Steph Curry is the greatest shooter God ever created."That's what I've always said. Here's the data.
1. Every shotAll 3.5 million NBA shots from 2009-10 to 2025-26, and exactly where on the court each one was taken.
2. The average shooterA computer program learned how often an average NBA player makes a shot from each spot. It never got to peek at the season it was judging.
3. Extra pointsWhat Curry actually scored, minus what an average shooter would have scored from the exact same shots. More extra points = better shooter.

The answer: since 2009, Steph has scored 3,188 extra points compared with an average shooter taking his shots. That's No. 1 in the NBA, ahead of Kevin Durant and Nikola Jokić.

Hard shots vs made shots

Further left = harder shots. Higher up = more points per shot. Anyone above the dashed line scores more than an average shooter would from the same shots. Bigger bubble = more shots.

Why the program only looks at where a shot was taken. NBA.com also writes notes on each shot, like "pull-up jumper" or "step-back". But back in the day, those notes were mostly written down when the shot went in: in 2009-10, threes marked "pull-up" went in 71% of the time, while the whole league made only 35.5% of its threes. Using those notes would be like peeking at the answer, so they're left out.
